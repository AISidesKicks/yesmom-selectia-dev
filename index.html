Intro

Some time ago I needed to steer an LLM in AI Harness to fix 3D models in code, or to start a 3D tool (takes ages) and edit it there.

So I was trying to create a YESMOM API with just a YES / NO logit, in AnyJev style.

Decision API

Selectia is a family of System One-style models fine-tuned from LFM 2.5, designed for one-pass typed decisions with calibrated probabilities. The Decision API (Choice, Noul, Score) is powered by open LLMs. For repo see: github.com AISidesKicks selectia.

Liquid AI LFM 2.5 models

They have a hybrid architecture and they are trained on a very large number of tokens in comparison with their weight.

The hybrid part is concrete: every layer block mixes short double-gated convolutions with plain GQA attention (the 2.6B model is 30 layers, 22 conv + 8 attention), which is why decode stays fast on CPU with a small memory footprint.

Training scale matches the claim: pretraining went from 10T to 28T tokens for the 1.2B family, the 2.6B checkpoint alone got 34T tokens, and post-training adds SFT rounds plus agentic reinforcement learning run inside real agent harnesses.

It is a whole edge family, not one size: 230M / 350M / 1.2B / 2.6B text models, an 8B-A1B MoE, plus VL, audio, encoder and retriever variants, with 128K context and 16 languages on the bigger text models.

Selectia fine-tunes exactly four of them: full typed decisions on the 2.6B and 1.2B, YESMOM (Noul only) on the 350M and 230M. Pretraining budget, vocabulary size and context window per base:

  • LFM2.5-2.6B-Base - 34T training tokens, vocabulary 128,000, context window 131,072 tokens (128K) on the model card (config says max_position_embeddings 128,000); the long length comes from a dedicated mid-training context-extension phase, the LFM2 predecessor shipped 32K, and rope stays type "default" so nothing is stretched at inference time
  • LFM2.5-1.2B-Base - 28T training tokens, vocabulary 65,536, context window 32,768 tokens
  • LFM2.5-350M-Base - 28T training tokens, vocabulary 65,536, context window 32,768 tokens
  • LFM2.5-230M-Base - 28T training tokens, vocabulary 65,536, context window 32,768 tokens

Runs where you need it: day-zero support for llama.cpp (GGUF), MLX, vLLM, ONNX and Liquid's own LEAP; about 220 tok/s decode on an M5 Max and 113 tok/s on a Ryzen CPU in under 2.5 GB of memory.

See also: Introducing LFM2.5 (liquid.ai), the LFM2.5 collection on Hugging Face, and the LFM2 technical report (arxiv).

Minus - OSS license with a commercial cap (tagged lfm1.0 on Hugging Face; free to download and fine-tune, but read the terms before selling on top of it).

So can we use LFM models to create our own Jev-like models?

Our Qwen models are distilled models. LiquidAI LFM models are built for embedded edge use cases, so they can take advantage of wider knowledge.

They are small enough so we can retrain them on 4070 / 4090 hardware, so a reasonably equipped PC gamer can experiment with them.

For repo see: github.com AISidesKicks selectia.

What is Jev

It's not a Decision per se model, it helps you make one (it's like IF but still not 100% predictable) - because it's still too random, so you must decide based on 3 classification types.

Jev is still a model, it's still just an AI model!

Benchmarks / JevBench

The map we use for decision models, plus what our own JevBench runs have taught us so far:

  • JevBench repo (github.com)
  • Jev models (benchmarkheaven.com)
  • Jev vs classic classifiers (mindstudio.ai)
  • Jev vs Claude Opus 5 classification (openrouter.ai)

Lesson 1: accuracy and calibration are different products. Our 2.6B base beat the 1.2B on JevBench public accuracy (0.541 vs 0.338) yet was worse calibrated on the hard tier (ECE 0.316 vs 0.210), so it is confidently wrong more often. One fitted temperature per checkpoint, around 2.3 to 2.5 on every base, cut ECE 2.5x to 3x without touching a single weight. A leaderboard number alone never tells you whether you can trust the probability that comes with it.

Lesson 2: more training is not automatically better calibration. The 55 minute teacher-only run doubled JevBench accuracy (0.338 to 0.628) but pushed the hard tier deeper into confident wrongness (ECE 0.210 to 0.343). Only the full 695,795 example mixture fixed it: overall ECE landed at 0.113, hard Brier matched the base again, and the fitted temperature fell from 2.50 to 1.55, which says the overconfidence was mostly gone. The held-out calibration gate in the plan earned its keep there.

Lesson 3: some tasks are shape-limited, not size-limited, and small slices are noise. The easy tier saturates at 1.000 after a short run, so it stops discriminating between models; meanwhile paws, tweet_hate, wic and msmarco_rel sit at or below chance for both 230M and 350M identically, which means the prompt shape fails, not the parameter count. JevBench also only publishes 231 of its 534 decisions, and only 74 of those are Noul, so accuracy on that subset moves plus or minus 0.11 and the thousands-item regression gates are where the real signal lives.

Jev-like systems with LLMs

Good map of the September clone wave: I read 7 clones of Jev (note.com). Short version below, each one next to decider. BERT-style encoder clones are left out on purpose.

decider - Mapika/decider (I choose this one) - family of Qwen3.5 fine-tunes (0.8B up to 35B MoE) trained on ~95 public decision datasets plus labels from a local 27B teacher, nothing distilled from Jev. One forward pass reads letter logits per question, serves TypeSafe's wire format, and the 2B got a calibration RL stage. It is the most complete "train your own" pipeline of the group; on JevBench the 35B sits mid-table because cost and calibration drag the geometric mean, same weak spots as the rest.

Kev - jaredpalmer/kev - small Qwen3.5 family (0.8B/4B/9B) with LoRA plus a pointer head and the only serious question isolation (block-causal mask, questions cannot see each other). Trained on a public ~10k-example suite with per-checkpoint temperature; 9B hits 0.852 on new sources vs Jev 0.857. vs decider: narrower data and no RL stage, but cleaner architecture fidelity to Archer Hume's writeup and a polished playground; decider wins on breadth and open training code.

SemIf - TheoLeeCJ/SemIf - no training: frozen Qwen3.5-4B (or smaller/bigger) reads option logits at request time, criteria defined per call, shared state prefetched once. JevBench #2 overall, but calibration (72.6) still well under Jev (82.7). vs decider: zero recipe to maintain and trivial to run, where decider ships a whole mixture + server; same "no generation" trick, opposite end of the effort spectrum.

Bespoke Nimble - bespokelabsai/nimble - Qwen3.5-9B + LoRA trained on only 2,676 contrastive pairs (near-identical examples, one fact flips the label) plus a fitted temperature. Scores 90.1% on its own 324-example holdout vs Jev 93.2%. vs decider: tiny but carefully curated data instead of decider's million-example epoch; great data-curation recipe, narrow domains, ~18 GB to run.

ruling - bradAGI/ruling - default is no training: any open chat model, state encoded once, option-letter logits averaged over 3 cyclic orderings to kill position bias, optional LoRA + cascade (can even escalate to typesafe:jev-latest). On TypeSafe's 102 rows the 35B scores 0.892 vs Jev 0.882 (difference not significant). vs decider: debiases after the read instead of training calibration in; Mac/MLX-first, one request at a time.

jevlike - vinnylarouge/jevlike - research starter: option-attention scorer over byte embeddings or a frozen encoder, not a full LLM fine-tune; ~98% on synthetic menus, 26-29% on Wikispeedia vs ~8% controls. Author says "research starter, not a copy of Jev". vs decider: teaches the readout idea cheaply, nowhere near production accuracy; the jevbetter follow-up trades ~115x throughput for better calibration.

NanoJev - TianyuCodings/NanoJev - Qwen3-0.6B with decision heads, open weights/data/pipeline, aimed at game and agent control (ViZDoom, maze, snake) with dynamic 2-255 candidates; beats Jev on some game tasks. vs decider: domain-specific control decisions rather than general judgment; smaller, task-tuned, not a Jev replacement for text routing.

Diffusions, Gemma style

Same survey also separates the diffusion clones properly: note.com clone review. Two real projects, both zero-training on DiffusionGemma - different bet from decider's trained causal models.

JoshuaSP/open-jev - JoshuaSP/open-jev - DiffusionGemma-26B denoises a fixed JSON canvas, then final logits pick allowed answer tokens; no training. On 337 public Jev questions: 86.1% (1 step) / 87.8% (2 steps) vs saved Jev 90.8%, with grouped 2-step ~4.85x faster / ~79% cheaper than naive per-question runs. vs decider: diffusion readout instead of one-pass letter logits, solid public-set accuracy, probabilities explicitly uncalibrated.

razorback16/openjev - razorback16/openjev - Jev-wire-compatible server on DiffusionGemma NVFP4 via vLLM (PR #57250) or MLX on Mac; adds images, steps, samples, think; TypeSafe SDK works unchanged; throughput numbers (up to ~57 req/s) but no published accuracy table. Apache-2.0. vs decider: infra/serving play around a diffusion backbone vs decider's trained weights; both drop into the same System One API.

Related how-to: How to build a Jev-style classifier with DiffusionGemma + vLLM (LinkedIn).
